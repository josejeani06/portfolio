The problem

Billing sat on the money itself

My part

The flows I designed

Structure

Billing groups and pooled balance

The org holds a total balance and, for enterprises, a monthly volume commitment. Admins create billing groups, nest groups inside them, and attach one or more payment methods.

Control

Payment access, budgets, and caps

Per-account Monthly Cap or No Limit, plus who is allowed to spend. Designed to go after the failure pattern behind the 14 tickets.

Movement

Transfers, disbursement, purchases

Move balance between a group and an account, split a volume commitment across billing groups, and add funds by card, bank, or invoice, all in-product.

Decisions

Where the platform pushed back

Each constraint became a decision you can see, with a path forward rather than a dead end.

QuestionTrade-offDecision
Should caps be mandatory?Hard caps stop overspend but could halt a paying campaign.Optional guardrails plus structural redundancy: multiple billing groups × multiple payment methods, so one declined card can’t take down a live campaign.
How do you change a payment method?Stripe won’t let a saved card’s number be edited in place.Make adding effortless. Every “change” is really adding a new method and repointing the automation.
When are new funds spendable?Show funds instantly, but they aren’t verified yet.Balance and receipt show at once but sit in Pending verification, unspendable until they clear.

Process

Money is where permissions collide

The org holds a total balance and a monthly volume commitment. Admins create billing groups, often one per campaign or region, then nest groups inside them, attach payment methods, and set how much each account can spend.

Every money action inherits the same rule as the rest of the org: no structural change without first showing what it breaks.

What I’d revisit

The one thing I’d change

Disbursement and resolving affected automations are powerful but still ask a lot of the admin. I’d collapse the multi-step money moves into fewer, clearer decisions while keeping every consequence visible.
